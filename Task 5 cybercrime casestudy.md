UPI (Unified Payments Interface) fraud is a type of cybercrime where scammers trick users into transferring money or revealing sensitive details like OTP or UPI PIN. These frauds usually rely on social engineering rather than technical hacking
A common scenario involves a victim receiving a call, SMS, or WhatsApp message pretending to be from a bank, electricity board, or customer service. The fraud typically happens step-by-step:

The scammer creates urgency (e.g., “Your account will be blocked” or “Pending bill payment”).
They send a fake payment request or malicious link.
The victim is asked to enter their UPI PIN or install a remote access app.
Once the PIN is entered, money is instantly transferred from the victim’s account.

For example, in one real case, a user received a fake electricity bill message and was tricked into installing an app, allowing fraudsters to access SMS and complete unauthorized transactions
These scams usually target students, elderly people, and new digital users who may not fully understand how UPI works.
The consequences can be severe, including financial loss, emotional stress, and difficulty recovering money. With the rise in digital payments, such fraud cases have increased significantly, causing major concerns for users and regulators.

In conclusion, UPI fraud highlights the importance of awareness, verification, and cautious digital behavior to ensure safe online transactions.
